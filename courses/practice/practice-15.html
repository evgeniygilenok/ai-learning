15. Learn from use

15 minutes explanation · 55 minutes building · 20 minutes review

Prerequisites: practice-14.

Record the result in module-3.md#use-observation.

What you will demonstrate

  • Observe three tasks without inventing user research
  • Measure one targeted product change against the same tasks

The before-and-after record supports a specific change and accurately labels peer review or self-review.

Understand the mechanism

Ask a peer to attempt an ordinary case, a rejected escalation, and a failed lookup. Watch where they hesitate, abandon the task, or correct the assistant manually. Ask what they thought would happen before they issue approve. Avoid coaching them through every decision.

If a peer is unavailable, conduct a structured self-review and label it honestly. Use the same tasks before and after the change. Counts and observations from one person support a local design decision, not a broad claim about users.

Worked example

A reviewer uses run twice because they cannot see that the case is waiting for a decision. Before the change, they need a reminder on two of three tasks. Add a pending-state message with the relevant show, approve, and reject commands. Repeat the tasks and record whether they still need a reminder. Do not change the task wording midway through the comparison.

Task | before confusion/correction | change | after observation
Ordinary case | observed, not assumed | one targeted edit | same task
Rejected case | observed, not assumed | same edit | same task
Failed lookup | observed, not assumed | same edit | same task

Run shell examples at the repository root. Commented sketches describe learner code to write; they are not additional installed commands.

Guided build · 30 minutes

  1. Prepare three synthetic task cards and decide what confusion or manual correction you will record.
  2. Observe a peer or labeled self-review without changing the implementation during the first pass.
  3. Make one targeted change and repeat the same cards. Record both remaining confusion and improvement.

Your task · 15 minutes

Explain an observation that did not improve. Decide whether the product change, the instruction, or the task definition should change next.

Failure investigation · 10 minutes

A smoother second attempt comes from remembering the commands rather than the product edit. State this confound and avoid claiming the edit caused the whole improvement.

Before running, predict the state, evidence, and effect count. Then compare your prediction with the record and name the responsible layer.

Exit explanation · 20 minutes

What did you observe directly, what did you infer, and what would a second reviewer help establish?

Explain the result in your own words and link the supporting command, code change, or redacted record. A missing prerequisite uses the next session's time.
